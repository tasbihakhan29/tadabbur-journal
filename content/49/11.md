This morning, as part of my daily habit of reading and reflecting on the Qur'an, I came across a beautiful verse from Surah Al-Hujurat. As I read its tafsir, I found myself reflecting on something profound.

The more I reflected, the more I realized that the Qur'an is not merely a book of commands and prohibitions. It is a guide to character. It teaches us how to interact with people, how to honour one another, and how to preserve human dignity.

Today, we live in a time where ridicule is often disguised as humour. Social media rewards mockery, sarcasm, and humiliation. People are turned into memes, strangers are judged based on appearance, and offensive comments are dismissed as "just jokes."

Women, in particular, are frequently objectified, mocked, or spoken about in ways that strip away the dignity that Allah has granted them. Yet this problem is not limited to women. Men, women, young people, and even entire communities can become targets of ridicule and contempt.

Then I looked again at the verse.

What struck me was that Allah does not merely prohibit mockery. He reminds us that the person we look down upon may actually be better than us in His sight.

How often do we judge people based on appearance, education, wealth, social status, or popularity? Yet Allah alone knows the reality of hearts. A person whom society overlooks may be far more beloved to Allah than someone admired by thousands.

The verse then goes even further. It teaches us not to insult one another and not to call people by offensive labels. In our time, labels have become powerful weapons. People are reduced to stereotypes, mocked for their mistakes, and defined by their past rather than their efforts to improve.

A careless nickname, a humiliating comment, or a public insult may seem small to us, but the Qur'an reminds us that words carry weight. They can either preserve dignity or destroy it.

What I find particularly beautiful is that Islam does not simply react to social problems after they appear. It nurtures values that prevent those problems from taking root in the first place.

By teaching humility, respect, haya, good character, and accountability in speech, Islam builds a society where human dignity is protected long before mockery, hatred, and division become normalised.

This verse reminds me that true faith is not only reflected in worship. It is also reflected in the way we speak about people, the way we think about them, and the respect we show them when no one is watching.

May Allah purify our hearts from arrogance, protect our tongues from harming others, and allow us to honour people in the way that He has commanded.

~ Tasbiha Khan
