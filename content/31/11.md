Reading the early ayat of Surah Luqman, I noticed how the surah eases into its lessons. It does not open with the famous advice Luqman gives his son. It first establishes something underneath that advice — the reality of who Allah is, demonstrated through creation itself.

"This is the creation of Allah. Now show Me what those besides Him have created."

It reads almost like a quiet, unanswerable challenge. Not aggressive, not defensive — just a simple invitation to look around. Every mountain, every cell, every star, every human fingerprint, traces back to a single source. And then the verse asks the obvious question nobody can actually answer: what has anything else created?

I found myself thinking about how much of modern life is built on borrowed materials, recombined and rearranged, and called "creation." Engineers, artists, scientists — myself included, as someone who builds software for a living — we shape and combine what already exists. We do not originate matter, life, or law. We work within a creation that was already there.

That distinction is the whole of tawheed in a single line. Not "Allah is powerful," but "Allah alone creates, and everything else merely rearranges."

What I appreciated is that this ayah comes right before Luqman's well-known counsel to his son. It is placed first deliberately, I think. Before Luqman teaches gratitude, before he teaches against shirk, before he teaches good character — the surah grounds the reader in this one fact: there is a Creator, and there is everything else.

I want to remember this the next time I am impressed by something built by human hands — a building, a piece of technology, a beautiful piece of art. Impressed, yes. But never confused about where the raw material of existence actually came from.

~ Tasbiha Khan
